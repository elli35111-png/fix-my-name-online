Step 3 of 4 · Your plan

Your plan · Old news story · Australia

Ask the publisher to update the story, then ask Google to catch up.

The story leaves out what happened next, so you have a clear and reasonable thing to ask for. Here’s the order to do it in.

Who decides

The publisher decides what’s on the page. Google decides what shows in search, and only updates once the page changes.

Time

About 30 minutes of your time. Allow 2 to 6 weeks for replies. Some publishers take longer, and some don’t reply.

Do these in order.Google’s refresh tool only works once the article has actually changed. Asking Google first, while the story is the same, will usually be turned down.

  1. Save your evidence

    5 minutes

    • The link, headline and date of the article
    • A screenshot with today’s date showing
    • The words you searched when you found it
    • Proof of what happened next, like a court outcome or letter

  2. Decide what to ask for

    5 minutes

    You can ask for more than one thing. Start with what matters most to you.

    Add an update about what happened nextThe smallest change you can ask for
    Remove or change my name in the story
    Stop the page showing in search enginesCalled a “noindex” tag. The page stays, but search engines drop it.
    Take the story down

  3. Send your letter to the publisher

    15 minutesYou send it

    Free letter · Publisher update request

    Subject: Request to update an article about me

    Dear Editor,

    I’m writing about this article on your site:
    link to the article
    Headline: headline · Published: date

    The article reports that what the article says, in one sentence. Since then, what happened next, e.g. the charge was withdrawn on 3 May 2021.

    I’m asking you to consider adding an update to the article so it reflects this outcome. I’ve attached the court outcome, letter or other document, and I’m happy to provide anything else you need to check it.

    The article still comes up when people search my name, and without the outcome it gives an unfair picture of what happened. I’d be grateful for a reply within 14 days.

    Kind regards,
    your name
    your email

    Where to send it: look for “Corrections”, “Contact the editor” or “Complaints” on the publisher’s site. Send it to one place and keep a copy.

  4. Wait, then follow up

    Check back on 28 October

    If there’s no reply after 14 days, send a short, polite follow-up. Your tracker will show it as due.

  5. Once the page changes, ask Google to refresh it

    5 minutes

    Paste the same link. Google checks the live page and updates or drops the old result.

    Open Google’s refresh tool How it works

  6. If the answer is no

    • If the publisher belongs to the Australian Press Council, you can complain there once you’ve tried the publisher. Press Council complaints
    • Australia’s privacy law generally doesn’t cover news reporting by media organisations, so a privacy complaint usually won’t fit a news story.
    • If the story is untrue and harming you, talk to a lawyer soon. Defamation claims in Australia generally have to be started within a year of publication.
